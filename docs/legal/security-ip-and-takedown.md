# Security, incident response, intellectual property, and takedown

Version and effective date: 2026-08-05

## Security reporting

Publish the configured security contact and a `security.txt` endpoint before public launch. Reports should include affected component, reproduction, impact, and safe contact details. Researchers must avoid privacy invasion, persistence, destructive testing, denial of service, extortion, social engineering, and accessing data beyond what is needed to demonstrate the issue. No bounty is promised unless a separate program says so.

## Incident process

Triage; contain; preserve integrity-protected evidence; assess data, users, locations, severity, and legal triggers; eradicate; recover from verified checkpoints; notify customers, people, insurers, processors, and regulators within applicable deadlines; document decisions; and complete corrective actions. Never promise a universal notification deadline—regional tests differ.

## IP and takedown

The operator respects copyright, trademark, privacy, publicity, confidentiality, and other rights. A notice should identify the protected work/right, challenged material and location, contact information, good-faith statement, accuracy/authority statement, and signature where required. The operator may remove or restrict material, notify the user, accept a valid counter-notice where law provides, and terminate repeat infringers under an adopted policy.

The US DMCA safe-harbor process requires a designated agent and Copyright Office registration before the site claims that status. Other jurisdictions require their own process. The operator must record the provenance and licence of project branding and third-party assets; uploading material is not itself proof of ownership.
